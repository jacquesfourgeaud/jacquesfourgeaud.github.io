Research

My full publication list is available on PubMed. Please get in touch if you have any access issues or would like to discuss a collaboration.

My research follows one continuous arc, from unbiased sequencing as a discovery tool, to the characterization of the viral diseases it reveals, to their translation into diagnostics and policy. It maps to three core themes:

Clinical metagenomics and pathogen discovery

Since 2019 I have built and now lead the clinical metagenomics program of Necker-Enfants Malades Hospital, which analyzes tissue biopsies, cerebrospinal fluid, amniotic fluid, stool and blood from patients with unexplained infectious syndromes, about half of them referred by rare-disease reference centers across the Paris region. The program received the national reference laboratory label (LBMR) for clinical metagenomics in 2024 and ISO 15189 accreditation in 2025.

Our three-year prospective evaluation of clinical metagenomics in France (The Lancet Microbe, 2024) is the reference study for the field in the country. The program has also produced the identification or first clinical description of several viral diseases: the first hepatotropic human circovirus, HCirV-1, in a heart-lung transplant recipient (co-first author, Emerg Infect Dis 2023, with an associated patent); chronic Aichi virus disease as a new clinical entity in patients with inborn errors of immunity (Clin Infect Dis 2023); occult rat hepatitis E virus (Rocahepevirus ratti) infection causing cirrhosis with recurrence after liver transplantation (Am J Transplant 2026); fatal pigeon paramyxovirus type 1 encephalitis investigated in a One Health framework (Emerg Infect Dis 2026); and human coronavirus OC43 encephalitis treated with remdesivir (J Med Virol 2025).

Current directions include targeted viral capture as second-line diagnostics, cell-free RNA approaches to unexplained hepatitis, and two nationally funded studies I coordinate or scientifically lead: SENTINEL (metagenomics of stool and urine in 120 immunocompromised patients) and MetaMFIU (metatranscriptomics of fetal and placental tissues in unexplained intrauterine fetal demise), both supported by ANRS-MIE competitive research grants.

Congenital CMV infection: from reference diagnostics to screening policy

As deputy head of the laboratory associated with the French National Reference Center for congenital CMV, I work at the reference level on maternal serology, prenatal diagnosis and neonatal diagnosis, and take part in a weekly national multidisciplinary fetal medicine board. My work in this area has focused on the markers that guide clinical decisions: neonatal predictors of long-term outcome in the multicenter Cymepedia cohort (Pediatrics 2024), the value of serial viral load monitoring in the first two years of life (J Pediatr 2023), and vertical transmission risk stratified by IgG avidity and valacyclovir treatment in 561 pregnant women (Clin Infect Dis 2025), a study that directly informed practice as France adopted universal first-trimester CMV screening in 2025.

I co-invented MyCMV, a decision-support algorithm for interpreting CMV serology in pregnancy, whose multicenter validation study I coordinate (NCT06694428). Through a collaboration with the UCL Great Ormond Street Institute of Child Health, I also study CMV genomic diversity in congenital infection using whole-genome sequencing.

Host and pathogen metatranscriptomics in unexplained fetal and perinatal disease

A substantial fraction of intrauterine fetal deaths and fetal anomalies remain unexplained after standard investigation. I coordinate METAHCN (metatranscriptomics of trophoblast biopsies) and scientifically lead MetaMFIU, two studies that apply RNA sequencing to fetal and placental tissues to detect both pathogens and host responses. During my research year in the Chiu laboratory at UCSF I am building the corresponding analysis pipeline, bridging the Necker cohorts with UCSF expertise in host-response genomics.

I am open to collaborations on any of these themes, particularly on sequencing-based diagnostics for immunocompromised patients, emerging viral infections, and maternal-fetal infections.
